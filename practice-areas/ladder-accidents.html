A Ladder Is a Safety Device, Not an Excuse to Blame the Worker

  1. Home

  3. A Ladder Is a Safety Device, Not an Excuse to Blame the Worker

Extension ladders, A-frame ladders, and makeshift access equipment can expose workers to serious elevation risks. A case may involve movement, collapse, an unsafe angle, a defective ladder, or the absence of a safer device.

How New York Law May Apply

Labor Law §240 may apply when a covered worker is not provided proper protection against an elevation-related risk. Labor Law §§200 and 241(6), common-law negligence, product claims, and other theories may also require investigation.

Potentially Responsible Parties

  • Owners and general contractors covered by the Labor Law
  • Entities directing or controlling the work
  • Companies that supplied, maintained, or selected defective equipment

Evidence That Can Make the Difference

  • The ladder itself, including feet, locks, spreaders, labels, and damage
  • Photographs of placement, surface, height, and tie-off points
  • Work orders, toolbox talks, safety records, and witness statements
  • Medical records documenting the first description of the accident

Defenses We Prepare to Meet

Insurers often contend that the ladder was sound, another device was available, the worker misused it, or the worker simply lost balance. The physical evidence and worksite layout can determine whether that story is plausible.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if no one saw the fall?

A claim is not automatically defeated. Prompt reporting, consistent medical history, scene evidence, and surrounding facts may establish what happened.

Should I keep the ladder?

If possible, do not permit disposal or alteration. Counsel can issue preservation demands.

What if the defense says I should have used another device?

That claim must be examined against what was actually available, where it was located, whether you were instructed to use it, and whether you had a valid reason not to.
