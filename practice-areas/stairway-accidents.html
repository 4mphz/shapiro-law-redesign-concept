Stairway Falls Require More Than a Quick Photograph

Broken treads, irregular risers, slippery surfaces, poor lighting, absent handrails, leaks, and unsafe cleaning can turn ordinary stairs into a serious hazard.

How New York Law May Apply

Owners and parties controlling property must use reasonable care under the circumstances. Building codes, notice, creation of the condition, and the relationship between the defect and the fall may be central.

Potentially Responsible Parties

  • Owners, landlords, and managing agents
  • Commercial tenants controlling the stairway
  • Cleaning, maintenance, or construction contractors in appropriate cases

Evidence That Can Make the Difference

  • Measurements of risers, treads, nosings, landings, and handrails
  • Lighting readings, bulbs, switches, and photographs from the user’s perspective
  • Cleaning logs, leak history, complaints, violations, and repair records
  • Shoes, clothing, and the earliest medical descriptions

Defenses We Prepare to Meet

The defense may deny notice, call the condition open and obvious or trivial, or claim the injured person simply missed a step. Objective measurements and maintenance records can change the case.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a building-code violation automatically prove the case?

Not automatically, but an applicable violation may be important evidence of negligence and causation.

What if I cannot identify the precise defect immediately?

Prompt investigation can compare your account, injuries, photographs, witnesses, and physical layout.

Can poor lighting support a claim?

Yes, when inadequate illumination contributed and the responsible party created it or had the required notice or duty.
