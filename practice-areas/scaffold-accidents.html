Scaffold Falls Demand Immediate Investigation

  1. Home

  3. Scaffold Falls Demand Immediate Investigation

Scaffolds are supposed to give workers a safe platform at height. When a scaffold collapses, shifts, lacks proper rails, or is built without adequate fall protection, the resulting injuries can be catastrophic.

How New York Law May Apply

Labor Law §240 requires covered owners, contractors, and their agents to furnish or erect safety devices that provide proper protection for covered elevation-related work. Liability depends on the work, the accident mechanism, the defendants’ roles, and whether a statutory violation caused the injury.

Potentially Responsible Parties

  • Property owners and qualifying lessees
  • General contractors and construction managers with statutory authority
  • Contractors or agents responsible for the work or safety equipment

Evidence That Can Make the Difference

  • Photographs and measurements of the scaffold before alteration
  • Daily logs, safety plans, contracts, permits, and incident reports
  • Harness, lifeline, rail, plank, and anchorage evidence
  • Coworker testimony and available surveillance video

Defenses We Prepare to Meet

Defendants frequently argue that the worker was recalcitrant, failed to tie off, ignored instructions, or was the sole cause of the fall. Those defenses must be tested against the actual equipment supplied, available anchorage, instructions, supervision, and physical configuration of the work.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does every scaffold fall qualify under §240?

No. Coverage depends on the work and facts, but many construction, repair, demolition, and related elevation accidents require close §240 analysis.

Can the defense blame me for not tying off?

They may try. The decisive questions include whether appropriate protection and a usable anchorage were actually supplied and whether the alleged conduct was truly the only cause.

Who should preserve the scaffold?

An attorney should promptly demand preservation from owners, contractors, employers, and other responsible parties.
