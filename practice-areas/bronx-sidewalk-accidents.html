A Sidewalk Defect Can Change a Life in One Step

  3. A Sidewalk Defect Can Change a Life in One Step

Raised flags, broken pavement, holes, cellar doors, tree-root displacement, ice, and construction defects can cause serious falls. Identifying the correct property and responsible party is the first challenge.

How New York Law May Apply

In New York City, Administrative Code §7-210 generally places responsibility for reasonably safe abutting sidewalks on many property owners, subject to important exceptions. Other rules may apply to owner-occupied residential property, municipal property, tree-related conditions, and special uses.

Potentially Responsible Parties

  • The abutting property owner
  • A tenant or contractor that created or controlled the defect
  • The City or another public entity in circumstances where statutory rules permit
  • Utilities or contractors whose work caused the condition

Evidence That Can Make the Difference

  • Exact photographs and measurements before repair
  • Property records, deeds, leases, permits, complaints, and violations
  • Weather and snow-removal records when ice is involved
  • Surveillance video and witness identification

Defenses We Prepare to Meet

Owners may deny notice, claim the defect was trivial, invoke a statutory exception, blame a tree or utility, or dispute where the fall occurred. Precise scene documentation is essential.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who owns the sidewalk?

Ownership alone does not answer responsibility. New York City’s statutory scheme, property type, special use, and defect source all matter.

What if the sidewalk was repaired quickly?

That is why photographs, measurements, witness information, and preservation requests should be obtained immediately.

Can a small height difference still be dangerous?

Yes. Courts evaluate the surrounding circumstances, not measurement alone.
