How New York Law May Apply

A rear driver generally must maintain a safe distance and respond reasonably to foreseeable traffic conditions. New York’s no-fault system and serious-injury threshold create separate medical and coverage issues.

Potentially Responsible Parties

  • Negligent drivers and vehicle owners
  • Commercial employers where applicable
  • Bus, livery, delivery, and fleet operators
  • Other drivers whose conduct caused the chain of events

Evidence That Can Make the Difference

  • Dash-camera and surveillance video in native format
  • Vehicle photographs, repair records, event data, and inspection
  • Prompt medical complaints, imaging, examinations, and treatment history
  • Traffic conditions and reasons for any stop

Defenses We Prepare to Meet

Typical arguments include sudden stop, minimal property damage, pre-existing degeneration, delayed treatment, and gaps in care. The legal and medical proof must answer each defense directly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does little vehicle damage mean no serious injury?

No. Vehicle damage is one piece of evidence and does not determine the forces on a particular person or the medical diagnosis.

What if I had prior back or neck problems?

A defendant may be responsible for aggravating a pre-existing condition, but medical comparison and causation proof are important.

Should the original video be requested?

Yes. Native files, metadata, full duration, camera perspective, and potential missing footage may matter.
