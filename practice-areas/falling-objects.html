Workers Below Need Protection From Objects Above

Construction material, tools, crane loads, walls, and debris can fall with enormous force. These cases require investigation of hoisting, securing, storage, overhead work, exclusion zones, and protective devices.

How New York Law May Apply

Labor Law §240 can apply when an object that required securing for the work falls because adequate protection was not provided. Labor Law §241(6), Industrial Code provisions, and negligence theories may provide additional grounds.

Potentially Responsible Parties

  • Owners, general contractors, and statutory agents
  • Hoisting, demolition, masonry, façade, or material-handling contractors
  • Entities responsible for securing loads or controlling overhead work

Evidence That Can Make the Difference

  • The object’s weight, dimensions, height, and intended use
  • Rigging, crane, hoist, delivery, and inspection records
  • Photographs, video, witness accounts, and site diagrams
  • Contracts identifying responsibility for overhead work

Defenses We Prepare to Meet

Defendants may characterize the falling object as an ordinary construction hazard, deny that it required securing, or claim it had already been permanently installed. The object’s purpose and the work sequence matter.

Frequently Asked Questions

Must the object fall from a great height?

Not necessarily. The analysis focuses on the elevation differential, force, work purpose, and whether securing or hoisting protection was required.

Can several contractors be responsible?

Yes. Contracts and actual site authority may identify multiple responsible entities.

What should be documented?

The object, its original position, rigging or supports, nearby work, warnings, and all changes made after the incident.
