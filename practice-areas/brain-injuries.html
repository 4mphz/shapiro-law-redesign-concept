A Brain Injury May Be Invisible and Still Transform a Life

  3. A Brain Injury May Be Invisible and Still Transform a Life

Brain injuries can affect memory, processing speed, mood, judgment, sleep, balance, independence, work, and family relationships. Proof must show the person, not just the scan.

How New York Law May Apply

Liability follows the underlying event, while damages require medical causation and careful documentation of functional change. Experts may include neurology, neuropsychology, rehabilitation, vocational, and life-care disciplines.

Potentially Responsible Parties

  • Drivers, property owners, contractors, medical providers, and others responsible for the underlying event

Evidence That Can Make the Difference

  • Emergency and neurological records, imaging, and testing
  • School, work, tax, and pre-injury medical records
  • Family and coworker observations of before-and-after function
  • Rehabilitation, life-care, vocational, and economic evidence

Defenses We Prepare to Meet

Insurers may point to normal imaging, prior psychological or learning issues, inconsistent performance, or return to some activities. A longitudinal functional record is often more informative than a single test.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can imaging be normal?

Yes. Diagnosis may rely on clinical history, testing, symptoms, and function as well as imaging.

Why gather old records?

They establish the baseline against which post-injury change is evaluated.

What is a life-care plan?

It is an expert projection of future medical, therapeutic, equipment, support, and related needs.
